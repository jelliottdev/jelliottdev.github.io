# Dukotah repository: experiment leads and evidence boundaries

Source reviewed at commit `f08b20b632d91209ddbaa747e8e2f23df0c78ba1`, not a moving branch. The complete Git tree lists 4,388 entries; the central ledger contains 172 entries. This was a targeted source/code review, not execution of the repository's experiments or an audit of every file. Downloaded-source SHA-256 hashes are recorded in `dukotah-source-manifest.json`.

The later [red-initial source audit](../research/dukotah-red-initial-audit-2026-10-22.md) is a narrow independent check of the repository's Round 22 illustration extractor. ## Claims not imported

Near-uniform frequencies, high entropy and unsuccessful bounded searches do not establish that the intended cipher uses a full-length independent pad. A finite battery failing to distinguish two constructions establishes only the observed behavior under that battery. The repository itself retracts the unconditional unsolvability verdict, but its remaining “OTP-class” framing still needs explicit model and identification limits.

Likewise, reproducing known solved pages validates those implementations and controls; it does not validate every unrelated search mechanism or every negative claim. Recovery power must be checked for the specific encoder, decoder, scoring model, language, sample size and search budget.

For transcription work, distinguish correct rune identity, segmentation, line completeness and independent provenance. The paper discloses residual ornate-initial and whole-line limitations. Do not replace Liberpedia's existing evidence boundaries with the broader shorthand that the transcription is simply closed.

## Integration rule

Group related encoder variants in one family and record exactly which assumptions changed. Keep source-reported outcomes as leads until freshly reproduced with pinned inputs and an independent check. Any final article should include a clear model diagram and a complete control table, with equal treatment of the languages. No hosted AI calls or large seed sweeps are necessary for this initial review.

## Planted decoder follow-up

The [decoder protocol](../experiments/plans/decoder-skip-controls.md) is frozen before local beam scoring. Encoder and transition controls now exhaust small three-symbol fixtures and check long rejection runs, rewrite wraparound, key exhaustion and an unrelated advance. A two-symbol ciphertext example also demonstrates that even the correct key can permit multiple plaintexts under key skipping; beam recovery must not be described as guaranteed inversion. The full 216-plant, 2,160-decoder benchmark now passes independent encoder-trace, candidate, score and beam-selection replay. The complete report and receipt are preserved under `experiments/records/decoder-skip-controls/`; the [public experiment account](../experiments/records/decoder-skip-controls/article.md), both key-fixture figures and the reproduction bundle now accompany them. Correct-key rigid addition recovers every plant. Value rewriting frequently passes the score threshold without exact recovery; long runs of key values expose the fixed skip bounds. These are planted-control findings, not conclusions about the manuscript cipher.

## Search-maximum follow-up

The [bounded-search protocol](../experiments/plans/search-maximum-controls.md) is frozen and executed: nine trace prefixes, 32 paired searches each, and nested dictionaries of 1, 4, 16, 64 and 256 derived keys. Independent reconstruction verifies all 147,456 wrong-key scores, 288 true-key controls, winner/tie rules and true-key inclusion counts. Null maxima rise with the search budget but none crosses the fixed -5.5 threshold in this grid; all included true keys win and recover exactly. These finite controls do not calibrate other key families or adaptive selection. The preserved report and receipt are under `experiments/records/search-maximum-controls/`; the public figures, full account and reproduction bundle now accompany them.

## Image-row coverage follow-up

The source review now distinguishes three stages instead of treating the repository as one transcription method:

- `round10/L1-template/read4.py` classifies candidate components from saved geometry and templates. Its classifier does not read canonical rune labels, but it consumes precomputed components and bands; this inspection alone does not establish their independence.
- `round19/T2/indel.py` probes insertion and deletion within supplied forced sequences. Its planted single-rune controls are not whole-line omission controls.
- `transcription/linecrop.py` has an image-only `detect_rows` helper: it uses the central 22–78% of the image, a grayscale threshold of 128, a 1% ink threshold, minimum active height 20 and merge gap below 12. Its separate command-line presentation pairs proposed bands with canonical lines by index and asks for visual mapping when counts differ. Thus it would be incorrect to say that every row-related helper in the repository depends on the canonical line count.

The paper itself leaves whole-line omissions outside its reported coverage. We have not rerun its classifier or verified its numerical accuracy/confidence claims. Hashes of these three inspected sources are retained in the source manifest. The [image-row coverage protocol](../experiments/plans/image-row-coverage.md) fixes a fresh eight-page control before detector execution. It requires an image-first reference inventory, all unmodified-baseline disagreements, unchanged-image omission controls, an independent interval implementation and visual overlays. The checked research record is now preserved in [image-row coverage](../experiments/records/image-row-coverage/README.md): 83 proposals match 43 of 65 annotated rune rows across eight selected pages. All 24 non-runic reference rows also receive proposals. Visual review attributes the remaining bands to merged rune rows and artwork. No manual reference was revised after detection. This bounds the fixed segmentation rule, not rune identity, actual missing lines or the source classifier accuracy. The [public experiment account](../experiments/records/image-row-coverage/article.md) includes all eight overlays and offline reproduction.

## Known-crib seed-search follow-up

The [frozen protocol](../experiments/plans/seed-crib-controls.md) tests two explicitly encoded hash generators, dictionaries of seeds 0–4095, included and excluded planted seeds, and correct versus incorrect cribs. The [preserved results](../experiments/records/seed-crib-controls/README.md) retain 8192 candidate streams and all 120 cells. Two-rune prefix matches occur even in the wrong-generator and incorrect-crib controls, but none of their false candidates predicts the complete withheld suffix. At four or more crib runes in these fixtures, included generating-pair searches retain exactly the true seed and the other controls retain none. This is a finite known-plaintext control, not evidence identifying LP’s generator or a universal minimum crib length.
