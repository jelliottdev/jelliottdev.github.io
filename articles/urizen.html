Urizen is a recurring character in William Blake’s poetry and designs. He matters to the cover discussion because the figure in The Ancient of Days is commonly identified with him. Recognizing a figure, understanding its role in Blake, and explaining its selection for a puzzle are separate questions.

Who is Urizen?

The Blake Archive’s “Urizen, Bound and Unbound” discusses him as a character created by Blake, whose appearances vary across works. Its reading connects him with making laws and imposing limits. In the Europe frontispiece, his compasses support an interpretation of creation through measurement and boundaries.

The same essay contrasts that image with appearances in The First Book of Urizen, where bodily restriction and objects such as tablets and a book change the emphasis. It also notes uncertainty in identifying some other figures as Urizen. A bearded figure or a bent posture alone is therefore insufficient to identify every instance.

Why he enters the cover discussion

The lower compasses panel guide follows the published identification of the cover fragment and links the complete museum composition. That museum record qualifies the figure’s identity as “probably Urizen.” The Archive essay uses the name directly. Preserve each source’s wording rather than making them sound equally definite.

The cover isolates a fragment and changes its orientation. It does not display the full narrative setting of Blake’s work. Before interpreting a hand, instrument or ray of light, compare the complete preserved cover with the source composition. The adjacent Newton fragment provides a second depiction of measurement, but a repeated instrument is not a numerical extraction rule.

A comparison with circumference

Blake’s language of limits makes a comparison with circumference in Liber Primus worth articulating. The comparison should begin with the actual uses: the manuscript links circumference with the inner voice, dependence, and shedding. The Archive’s interpretation concerns a character’s role in Blake’s works. These are related subjects for reading, not interchangeable definitions.
