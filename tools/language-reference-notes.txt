Language-pattern comparison

Question: does the order of these runes resemble reference prose more than
32 rearrangements of the same runes? This is a diagnostic, not a readable-text
verdict, language identification or decryption test.

Eight ordinary references: English, Welsh, German, French, Spanish, Italian,
Portuguese and Finnish. Each model uses the same 997 FLORES dev sentences.
Each scores groups of three rune units, including groups across word breaks
but not across pages. Scores must not be compared directly across languages.
The fixed tables, spelling conversion, source hashes and license attribution
are in language-model.json. Derived statistics are CC BY-SA 4.0.

Reference spelling conversion
Latin references are uppercased and accents removed. Internal apostrophes
are removed; other non-A-Z characters separate words. K and Q become C,
V becomes U, and Z becomes S. The declared greedy groups map to GP29 units;
ING becomes NG and IO becomes IA. Words are then joined before scoring.
This mechanical conversion is not a phonetic transcription. It can distort
languages differently. Native manuscript rune outputs need no re-encoding.

What the controls showed
Held-out material uses eight disjoint source-article groups from FLORES
devtest, at lengths 40, 80, 160 and 240. No source URL occurs in both training
and devtest. Nine known LP readings are additional checks, not training data.
Correct texts outscored all 795 paired wrong-key outputs under the intended
reference. However, 88 of 768 wrong FLORES outputs and 4 of 27 wrong LP
outputs beat all 32 rearrangements under at least one reference. A low bar
in the interface therefore does not establish that output is readable.

The wrong-key controls cover only three errors in one encryption family.
They do not estimate the false-positive rate of a large key search. The
modern translated prose, small set of articles and spelling conversion limit
what these comparisons can establish. Forty runes is a declared minimum,
not a proven reliability threshold. Thirty-two rearrangements give coarse
counts, not probabilities, and do not account for selecting the best of
many attempted rules. A promising result needs coherent extended output and
a separately specified check on material not used to select the rule.

Reproduction from the repository
python scripts/build_language_model.py
node scripts/test_language_score.cjs
python scripts/calibrate_language_score.py --output PATH_TO_NEW_REPORT.json

language-reference-calibration.json retains the protocol, source/script
hashes and summary counts. The command above reproduces the full per-sample
report. Browser result packages contain the exact pinned language-model.json
and an independent Python implementation; downloading is optional.
