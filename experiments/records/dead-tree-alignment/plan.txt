Question: Does a fixed-scale, translated, horizontally reflected crop of the credited Wikimedia tree photograph correspond to the pale tree in LP2-55 more closely than the original orientation?

Declared crop, [left,top,right,bottom), native pixels:
  source [100,500,1750,1950]; target [550,1300,1850,2550].
Both grayscale. Source response clip((170-L)/130,0,1); target response clip((250-L)/45,0,1). Resize source response with OpenCV default linear interpolation; blur both with Gaussian sigma 7. Compare original orientation and one horizontal reflection; no rotation or shear. Scales: 0.370, 0.385, ..., 0.700 (23 values). Search every integer translation for which the whole resized source crop fits within the target crop. For each scale/translation maximize OpenCV TM_CCOEFF_NORMED either over the full source crop or its upper 60%. For the upper-fit variant, record Pearson correlation on the lower 40% at the selected transform, with no refit. The latter is a within-image check, not an untouched experimental holdout.

Disclosure: The choice of photograph, cropping windows, response clipping, blur and scale range followed visual inspection. Comparing the two orientations and lower-region score is exploratory. No null distribution or probability of chance matching is claimed. It tests geometric correspondence, not the exact authoring tool or processing sequence. A simple affine alignment cannot explain all missing/added branches and tonal choices.
