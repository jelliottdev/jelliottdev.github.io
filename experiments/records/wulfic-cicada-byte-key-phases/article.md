# Can archived Cicada numbers and byte streams serve as LP2 keys?

**Finding.** In this exact Stage R search, none of the 43,500 source settings reached the declared +30 detection score. The strongest LP2 score was **+2.41** on a nine-rune title section, but that section is **too short to reach +30 with this scorer under any key**. Among the other sections, the best was **−50.75**; the complete joined stream peaked at **−1,479.67**. All 200 planted positive controls exceeded +30 and all 200 wrong-key controls stayed below it. This is a result about the specified detector and inputs, not a claim that these sources cannot be used in another construction.

![Three detection-score extrema against the source pass line.](experiments/records/wulfic-cicada-byte-key-phases/comparison.svg)

The figure shows the lowest positive-control score, the highest LP2 score, and the highest negative-control score. Each point is an extremum of a different group, not one representative trial. The LP2 maximum comes from the title section, whose score ceiling is below the pass line.

## What was tested

The [pinned Stage R program](https://github.com/Wulfic/Cicada3301-Liber_Primus/blob/bf89d3d13aad2b8f2c95ab195cf1b092f250775c/tools/run_stage_r.py) collected 25 numerical or byte sources connected to earlier Cicada material. They include the published prime product and RSA modulus as decimal digits, cookie values, a source hash, two hex strings, a hint, and several archived byte files. A file name such as `page_17.bin` is the archive's label for a candidate key file; it is not itself proof of a Liber Primus page number or of an authenticated hidden payload.

Each source became two GP29 key streams: values reduced modulo 29, and values above the largest divisible range rejected before reduction. For each of those 50 streams, the test tried three arithmetic modes (`sub`, `add`, `beaufort`) and all 29 shifts against nine source sections separately and their continuous join. This gives **50 × 3 × 29 × 10 = 43,500** settings. Source section labels 7–15 refer to that program's transcript divisions; they are not manuscript page numbers. Together they cover the unsolved LP2 text through page 55, with some section boundaries inside pages.

The scorer allows the key stream to stay, advance once, or advance twice between runes, with probabilities 0.02, 0.96 and 0.02. It averages over **every** possible cyclic starting phase instead of choosing the most flattering one. It compares decoded-rune frequencies with those of the solved LP1 text and reports a log likelihood ratio; **+30** was the source's fixed pass line.

The nine-rune title accounts for **4,350** of the 43,500 settings. Its largest possible per-rune emission ratio is 3.467, so even a perfect path cannot exceed **9 × ln(3.467) = 11.19 nats**. The +30 line is mathematically unreachable there. The longer sections can reach the line in principle, though the planted controls test detection power only at 729 and 3,316 runes. Neither kind of evidence makes all 43,500 settings equally informative.

## What makes the screen interpretable

The source first encrypted solved LP1 text with each key under its own stay/advance/skip model, then measured the correct key and an independent wrong key. It used two lengths (729 and 3,316 runes) and two anti-doublet rekey settings. That produced 200 positive–negative pairs. The weakest correct-key score was **+169.09**; the strongest wrong-key score was **−60.86**. The positive controls show that this detector can recover keys generated by its own model at the tested lengths. They do not establish equal power against every possible real encryption scheme.

Our separately written compiled forward-sum implementation agreed with the pinned original scorer on 108 real cells spanning short and long source keys, short and joined LP2 sections, all three modes, and multiple shifts (largest absolute difference **2.27 × 10⁻¹² nats**). Synthetic equal-mass and skewed-emission cases check ties and pruning. It preserved the source control generator and random seed. The replay then checked every rounded candidate and control row against the two original source tables. These checks verify the computation; they do not establish that any candidate source was the actual LP2 key.

## Read or rerun the data

- [Full source candidate table](experiments/records/wulfic-cicada-byte-key-phases/source-stage_r_candidates.tsv) — one row per setting.
- [Source control table](experiments/records/wulfic-cicada-byte-key-phases/source-stage_r_controls.tsv) — 200 positive–negative pairs.
- [Packaged numeric inputs](experiments/records/wulfic-cicada-byte-key-phases/source-profile.npz) — the exact 50 key streams, section runes, and emission ratios used by the checker.
- [Result summary](experiments/records/wulfic-cicada-byte-key-phases/results.json) and [declared plan](experiments/records/wulfic-cicada-byte-key-phases/plan.json).

Run `python experiments/records/wulfic-cicada-byte-key-phases/check.py` to verify full-table hashes, controls and a spread of independently computed cells. Add `--full` to recompute all 43,500 LP2 scores from the packaged numeric inputs. To repeat source construction and planted controls from the pinned checkout, use `replay.py --source PATH --workers 4 --output PATH`; it writes only to the given output directory. The compiled scorer needs NumPy and Numba. `plot.py --check` verifies the figure against the result summary.

This screen does not test other starting distributions, source preprocessing, key schedules, language models, interruption probabilities, or later cipher layers. A proposal using one of these sources with a different specific rule remains a separate test.
