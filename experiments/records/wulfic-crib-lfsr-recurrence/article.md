# Can a guessed word reveal a repeating feedback rule?

**Result:** Correcting two problems in a [public crib-and-LFSR program](https://github.com/Wulfic/Cicada3301-Liber_Primus/blob/bf89d3d13aad2b8f2c95ab195cf1b092f250775c/tools/legacy/lfsr_alberti_solver.py) did not produce a strong continuation on the 11 LP2 pages that program named. The best score was **18** on LP2-05; **126 of 199** shuffled-page searches scored at least as high. A separate known-reading plant scored **119** and ranked first across an 11-page mixed grid, showing that this exact search can find a clean example of the proposed rule. It does not exclude other LFSR constructions.

![On a planted message, only the corrected rune reader and aligned key recover all 128 following runes. The best LP2 score is below the middle of the shuffled-search results.](experiments/records/wulfic-crib-lfsr-recurrence/result.svg)

The idea is concrete: suppose a word such as **EMERGE** appears somewhere in the ciphertext. Under an additive cipher, that guess reveals a short piece of the key. A linear feedback rule might then continue the key. We tried every placement of **13 source words**, three arithmetic directions and feedback lengths **2–5** on the source's **11 unsolved LP2 pages**. We learned a rule from the first part of each guessed word, required it to predict the last two key values under that word, then decrypted and scored **128 runes after the word**. Those 128 runes, not the guessed word, determine the score. Across **52,527 placements**, 13 candidate fits passed the two-rune recurrence check. They are not 13 distinct key rules: the additive and subtractive directions can represent the same decoded text with negated keys. The highest score came from an EMERGE guess at LP2-05 rune 62 in the Beaufort direction.

The original program's separate scan used **21 shorter-or-longer cribs** on 12 archived page files, including the solved LP1-15, and reported candidates only when its **29× normalized whole-page index of coincidence** exceeded 1.4 (a uniform-rune reference is about 1). We replayed **174,141 placements** and reproduced its **zero gated candidates**; the unchanged source function agreed on all 12 page counts. That zero is a fact about that program, not a sound exclusion of the proposed cipher. Its rune reader drops the manuscript's J glyph (`ᛄ`) because it expects a different glyph (`ᛂ`), losing **1–13 runes per page**. It also starts a key inferred at a later crib as if the crib were at page position zero. A known planted message isolates the errors: the source reader and source key position recovered only **6 of 128** following runes; fixing both recovered **128 of 128**. Twelve more planted cases recovered the full continuation for every arithmetic direction at feedback lengths 2 and 3; twelve additional endpoint plants did so at lengths 4 and 5.

The comparison shuffled each LP2 page's rune order once per trial and repeated the **entire page, word, direction and position search** with the same 199 trial seeds. Its median best score was **19**, and its highest was **40**. Page shuffling preserves rune totals but not the manuscript's word structure, so this is a conditional search control rather than a cipher simulation. The result does not rule out another word, longer feedback, a different key start, interruptions, a better language model or a full-page plaintext revealed by other evidence.

**Power check, added after the LP2 result:** The existing decoded rune text of LP1-14 contains **CIRCUMFERENCE**, one of the 13 original source words, at rune 52 with at least 128 known runes after it. We encrypted that **unchanged 226-rune reading** with planted page-local keys for all three directions and lengths 2–5. Every planted case recovered the next 128 runes exactly. On the planted page alone, the real candidate scored **119** and ranked first in all 12 searches (sharing first with an equivalent sign-form candidate in eight, not a competing plaintext). No F runes were inserted or replaced. The separate [power plan](experiments/records/wulfic-crib-lfsr-recurrence/power-plan.json) and [single-page results](experiments/records/wulfic-crib-lfsr-recurrence/power-results.json) preserve that first check.

**Full-grid power amendment, also after the LP2 result:** We replaced the first of the 11 tested page slots (LP2-11, **273 runes**) with the 226-rune known-message plant, leaving the other ten LP2 pages and the entire crib, direction and position search unchanged. Across **50,694** examined placements per planted arm, the true candidate still ranked first in all 12 mixed-grid searches. The ten unchanged pages contributed 13 eligible recurrences and a best score of 18, so none outranked or tied the planted score of 119. The [mixed-grid plan](experiments/records/wulfic-crib-lfsr-recurrence/power-grid-plan.json) was added after the single-page power review; [results](experiments/records/wulfic-crib-lfsr-recurrence/power-grid-results.json) give each arm's rank and ties. This shows detection power for a clean 226-rune known reading in a grid containing ten actual LP2 pages. Because the replaced page is shorter, the **199 original LP2 shuffles are not a calibrated null for this mixed grid**; their maximum of 40 is only background context. This check cannot establish the unknown LP2 plaintext style or exclude other feedback rules.
