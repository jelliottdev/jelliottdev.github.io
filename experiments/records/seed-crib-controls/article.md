# Does matching the opening text identify a seed?

A short readable opening can have several explanations. This experiment plants a known 95-rune passage under two fixed keystream generators, searches a finite seed dictionary, and asks whether seeds that reproduce the opening also predict the remaining text. No candidate is selected by looking at that remaining text.

A **crib** is an assumed exact plaintext sequence at an exact position. Here the crib is supplied as part of the control. It is not guessed from the ciphertext or inferred from a language score. The **withheld suffix** is everything after the crib: the part reserved for checking a prediction.

With two crib runes, every tested search retains several seeds. Only the true generating pairs recover the complete passage. At four or more crib runes, those generating pairs are the only survivors in the searches that include them; the other controls retain none. This is a result for these fixtures and dictionaries, not a general four-rune requirement.

## What is searched?

The plaintext is the 95 output rune indices from the known reading of LP2-57. New ciphertexts are generated from it. This is not a new attempt to decrypt the original LP2-57 image, and it supplies no claim about the unsolved pages.

The two generators use SHA-256 and SHA-512. Each hashes the ASCII domain separator `liberpedia-seed-control:v1:`, followed by a four-byte unsigned big-endian seed and a four-byte unsigned big-endian counter starting at zero. Digest bytes of 232 or greater are discarded; the remaining bytes are reduced modulo 29. Digests are concatenated until 95 indices are available. There are no skips, offsets, resets or alternate signs.

Each generator plants seeds 17, 4095 and 4096. Each search tests seeds **0 through 4095**, inclusive, under one generator. Seed 4096 is deliberately absent from both search dictionaries even though it is a valid generator input. Thus an unsuccessful search can simply mean the needed seed is outside its bounds.

Encryption is `cipher[i] = (plain[i] + key[i]) mod 29`. A seed survives when it reproduces every assumed crib rune at the start. Every survivor is retained; there is no English score, best-candidate ranking or phrase selection. Its complete decoded sequence is then compared with the known plaintext, keeping full-passage equality and withheld-suffix equality separate.

## Results

The figure reports the range of candidate counts across the searches in each control group. These ranges are not confidence intervals. The incorrect-crib group changes only the first rune by adding one modulo 29, leaving the rest of the assumed opening unchanged. It includes both matching and mismatched search generators.

| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Correct crib; generating pair included | 4 | 4, 6, 11, 8 | Exactly 1 per search |
| Correct crib; matching generator but seed outside range | 2 | 5, 4 | 0 |
| Correct crib; wrong generator | 6 | 8, 5, 10, 6, 8, 2 | 0 |
| Incorrect first crib rune | 12 | 6, 5, 2, 7, 4, 3, 8, 6, 7, 4, 4, 6 | 0 |

At length two, the four included-pair searches contain 29 survivors in total. Four are the true generating pairs; the other 25 fail to predict the complete 93-rune withheld suffix. All survivors in the other three control groups also fail that complete suffix test. They may agree with individual withheld runes, which is why the full report preserves both exact equality and the number of correct positions.

For all longer tested cribs, each included-pair search retains exactly the true seed, which recovers all 95 runes. An incorrect first crib rune prevents full-passage equality by construction; the independently evaluated suffix test is a separate check.

The complete summary retains all **120 cells**, including zero-survivor cases. The full JSON also stores every searched keystream, every planted ciphertext and every surviving candidate's decoded sequence. A generating-pair identity and an exact finite-stream match are conceptually different; no alternative pair recovers the whole passage in these controls.

## What an empty search establishes

An empty result excludes only the tested generator/seed pairs under the exact crib and alignment. It does not exclude seeds outside the dictionary, other generators, another seed encoding, a different crib, an unknown offset or a different cipher mechanism. The two omitted-seed fixtures demonstrate this directly: their ciphertexts are generated by a tested generator, yet the bounded correct-crib searches retain no seed once the crib reaches four runes.

These cells reuse one plaintext and fixed dictionaries. They are dependent controls, not independent samples from a population of ciphers. Short prefixes can match accidentally; no probability that an LP proposal is correct follows from these counts. Random-looking ciphertext also does not establish an independent one-time pad.

## Reproduce and inspect

Extract this experiment's ZIP with folders intact, or use the repository. Python 3 and its standard library are sufficient; no additional corpus, original-image download, API or model is needed.

```sh
python experiments/records/seed-crib-controls/run.py --check
```

The command reconstructs the result, independently checks every stream and survivor, and compares the complete summary and figure. Use `--output seed-replay` instead to retain newly generated reports in a new directory. The plot can also be regenerated with `python experiments/records/seed-crib-controls/plot.py`.

The protocol was committed before candidate generation. The checker imports no producer code: it separately packs seed/counter bytes, uses division to implement rejection conversion and checks compatibility by directly encrypting the assumed crib. Both implementations share Python's hash primitives. Boundary and exhaustive toy controls test byte order, the 231/232 cutoff and the difference between prefix, suffix and whole-text agreement. Human review remains pending.
