# A correct key can fail in the wrong decoder

The [Round 19 I1 source](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/tree/f08b20b632d91209ddbaa747e8e2f23df0c78ba1/liber-primus/analysis/round19/I1) encrypts known English-derived rune text with several ways of skipping key draws, then supplies the **correct key** to ten decoders. If a decoder cannot follow a planted message, its failure on an unknown LP2 key would tell us little.

![The source's original beam passes none of its twenty gated construction and length groups. Several permissive drift modes pass all twenty.](experiments/records/round19-decoder-repair-controls/decoder-repair-controls.svg)

**Finding:** On one 240-rune paired-skip message (seed 0), the original beam recovered **45.8%** of runes with the correct key; a permissive drift decoder recovered **100%** from that same message and key. Across the source's twenty gated construction-and-length groups, the original beam passed **0** and the permissive decoder with cost 8 passed **20**. Its cost-4 sibling also passed twenty; other exact or paired settings passed fewer. A group pass means the median of seven seeded messages met both exact-rune recovery of at least 90% and a language score of at least −5.5. It does not mean every message was fully recovered. The pictured seed was selected for explanation after the source results were known; the full grid supports the broader comparison.

The 2,100 decodes cover **15 constructed cases**, ten decoder settings, two lengths (240 and 400 runes), and seven seeds. Ten constructions per length belong to the source's positive gate; the baseline and four extra probes are measured separately. The constructions include paired skipped draws, free drift, occasional pointer jumps, key-dependent two-draw decisions, and keys with long repeated runs. Every result uses synthetic ciphertext with the known construction and correct key supplied. This is an instrument-power test, **not an LP2 decryption**. The separate [full-book control](experiments/records/round19-full-book-decoder-power/article.md) tests two rules at 12,956 runes; it does not establish full-length power for all ten rules here.

The exact source command was replayed without changing its functions or its pinned Self-Reliance text. The [frozen retrospective plan](experiments/records/round19-decoder-repair-controls/plan.json) demanded bit-exact non-timing JSON. **That exact condition failed:** 88 of 2,100 row scores and eight of 300 summary score fields differ at the final floating-point bits, by at most 2.665×10⁻¹⁵ and 1.777×10⁻¹⁵ respectively. The cause of those tiny differences is not established. All other non-timing fields, construction/seed coverage, group decisions and skip limits match exactly. A [post-result comparison](experiments/records/round19-decoder-repair-controls/precision-addendum.json) bounds the score differences at 3×10⁻¹⁵; it does not change the original acceptance rule.

Allowing more key-pointer movement helps these known-key plants, but it also creates more ways for a **wrong key** to fit. This replay does not run wrong-key controls for all ten modes. The [separate Round 19 null-bar audit](experiments/records/round19-decoder-null-bars/article.md) examines that calibration question. A language score or 90% recovery gate here does not give a false-positive rate for unknown keys, other languages, other message lengths, or the actual LP2 cipher. The complete case grid and source bytes are included so a different decoder rule can be checked without treating these 2,100 settings as 2,100 independent ideas.

From the extracted experiment folder, run `python experiments/records/round19-decoder-repair-controls/run.py --check` to verify saved source/replay rows and independently recompute all 300 group summaries. To repeat the 2,100 decodes, run `python experiments/records/round19-decoder-repair-controls/run.py --nproc 4`. The runner copies the pinned source into a temporary directory and leaves the archived output unchanged. Use `python experiments/records/round19-decoder-repair-controls/plot.py --check` to verify the chart.
