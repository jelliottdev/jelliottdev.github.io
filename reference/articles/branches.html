Branching forms occur in both releases. They differ in placement and appearance: a narrow drawing beside the text on LP2-08–14, curled borders later in the manuscript, and a pale tree-shaped drawing inside LP2-55. Keeping these examples separate makes it possible to compare them without losing their page context.

The leafy border on the Welcome pages

LP1-03 and LP1-04 place their text inside thick curving branches, with dense leaf shapes above and more open loops along the sides. Branches turn inward beneath the text. The broad arrangement is shared across the two sheets; describing that resemblance is not a pixel-level claim that every line is identical.

The Welcome reading spans both pages. Its language of pilgrimage and emergence gives textual context, but does not identify the plant forms or establish that the border encodes a route. Compare these enclosing branches with the separate margin drawing on LP2-08–14 below.

The small instruction at the bottom of LP1-04

Just above the lower border, LP1-04 has a line of red runes much smaller than the main writing. The known reading gives AN INSTRUCTIAN. COMMAND YOUR OWN SELF. This is visible text on the page, not an extracted hidden message.

Use the complete reading and reproduction instructions to check how the line is decoded. Keeping it with the preceding text avoids losing a short instruction simply because it falls outside the main block. The comparison of self-direction instructions places this sentence beside LP1-13 and LP1-16. The red-writing guide explains why color and placement should be recorded separately from rune values.

The narrow branching drawing on LP2-08–14

LP2-08 shows a long, slender form along the left margin. Smaller lines branch off toward its upper and lower ends, while the main text sits to its right. LP2-14 shows the same broad arrangement and includes an insect drawing below the text at the right.

The community groups LP2-08–14 under “Branches.” Open the middle pages to compare placement and linework: 09 · 10 · 11 · 12 · 13. The group name does not establish that the form represents a particular plant species, diagram, or cipher key.

The branch changes sides on successive pages

Across this seven-page group, the branch alternates between the left and right margins. Read the sequence in archive order:

A drawing can move to the other margin without being mirrored. To test reflection, compare corresponding tips, junctions, and bends after aligning the images; the left/right sequence alone does not answer that question. Likewise, a proposal to reverse text on alternating pages would be an additional operation to test, not something already demonstrated by this layout.

Curled borders and the pale tree

On LP2-40, branching forms are part of the curled drawings on both sides of the text. LP2-55 has that border style as well as a much lighter tree-shaped drawing in the central space above the insect. Its low contrast is itself a reason to consult the original image rather than relying on a transcription.

Compare the spiral and branching borders, or use the illustrated index to browse the other artwork groups, including the separately named LP2-27–32 “Wing & Tree” group.

The Wing and Tree group adds another distinct example: the soft-edged tree-shaped figure on LP2-32. It should not be conflated with the pale, more finely outlined drawing on LP2-55.

Compare the two central tree drawings

These close views use equally wide regions of the original images. LP2-32 has broad, dark forms with soft edges. LP2-55 has much lighter, outlined contours with enclosed white spaces. The difference is already present in the preserved JPEGs; neither view has been sharpened or given extra contrast.

Both drawings have a trunk-like base and branching forms, but that resemblance does not yet establish reuse of one image. A proposed match needs corresponding bends, junctions, and proportions, with any transformations stated. Nor can this comparison tell us why one drawing is faint or the other soft-edged.

The page records offer an Inspect control for each tree. The selected rectangles are [650, 2050, 1100, 1150] on LP2-32 and [650, 1400, 1100, 1100] on LP2-55, in original-image pixels. They locate the drawings for inspection; they are not branch-count measurements.

What does “count the branches” mean?

A branch count sounds straightforward until two people trace the same drawing differently. One may count every visible line leaving a trunk; another may count terminal tips; a third may count each segment between junctions. Those are different measurements.

QuantityA possible explicit definition
TipsEndpoints of a traced line, excluding the boundary of the chosen crop
JunctionsLocations where three or more traced segments meet
SegmentsPaths between tips or junctions, with no junction in their interior
Main stemA selected path identified by its endpoints and a stated selection rule

Under a graph representation, tips and junctions become vertices and the connecting segments become edges. A visually tree-like drawing need not yield a mathematical tree: a crossing may or may not be a connection, and a closed loop would change the graph. Publish an annotated tracing so another person can see those choices.

Handling the pale drawing on LP2-55

Increasing contrast can make a faint line easier to see, but it also changes the displayed pixels. Preserve the original file, label an enhanced image as a derivative, and record the operation. If a claim depends on a feature visible only after enhancement, show the corresponding unmodified region too.

For numerical work, choose the brightness threshold before counting and check how modest changes affect the result. A count that changes sharply with the threshold needs that sensitivity reported. The existing image-region guide explains thresholds used for other published measurements; those settings have not been validated as a segmentation method for this pale tree.

From appearance to interpretation

A botanical identification, a religious association, and a decoding instruction each require different evidence. A comparison with a named artwork should show the proposed source image and the matching features. A cipher proposal should specify how the drawing produces an ordered sequence or operation, rather than selecting a count because it happens to fit.

For wider interpretation, start with philosophical themes in the known readings. That article distinguishes what the text says from suggested connections. No particular symbolic meaning is assigned to these branching drawings here.

Paired plant borders on LP2-41–44

These four sheets each have twelve rows of black runes between tall curling stems. Look for the drooping narrow leaves, the root-like lower branches, and the groups of filled dots beside the stems. The page records provide original-image inspection regions: 41, 42, 43, and 44.

A related border arrangement also frames the alphanumeric grids on LP2-49–51. This gives a visual comparison across different text layouts. It does not by itself establish identical artwork files, a plant species, or a shared cipher.

Keep the border dots separate from marks within the text. LP2-42 has paired short vertical strokes near the right ends of rows three and six; LP2-43 has a pair near the end of row one. Those observations are recorded on the individual pages. Their resemblance to familiar punctuation is not enough to assign a grammatical role; the punctuation guide explains how to preserve visual distinctions in an analysis.

The runic pages around the grids

The same broad border arrangement continues on LP2-45, 46, 47, and 48. Each has twelve runic rows. LP2-49–51 then place alphanumeric arrays inside the borders, before LP2-52 returns to a full twelve-row runic block.

PagesCentral content
LP2-45–48Twelve runic rows on each sheet
LP2-49–51Alphanumeric grids; runic text above the first and below the last
LP2-52Twelve runic rows

That sequence is useful when defining a test's scope. A proposal based on the grid entries should say whether it also uses the surrounding runes or border marks. The change in central content does not by itself demonstrate a new key, cipher, or instruction to reset an operation.

There are also smaller differences worth inspecting. LP2-47 ends its first row with a dotted cluster. On LP2-48, small dots precede the first runes of rows four and six. The page records locate these examples in the originals; neither their placement nor the shared border assigns them a decoded meaning.
