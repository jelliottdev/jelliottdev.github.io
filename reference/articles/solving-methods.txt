Solving methods: a map of families and variants

Browse overlapping method and diagnostic families, then narrow an idea to a reproducible test.

Start with the kind of operation an idea proposes, then narrow it to a specific rule and test. This map includes decoding methods, ways to search for keys, and diagnostic checks. They overlap: a proposal can combine a route, a numerical key and a reset rule. Combining families adds choices that must be counted.

A family page is not a completed experiment. Open its guide to understand the method, or expand its linked records to compare the exact questions tested. The records cover only their stated inputs, variants and bounds. No linked record means a gap in this map, not an untouched or promising approach.

What the record types mean
Manuscript analysis
Tests or measures declared manuscript inputs; read the scope for pages, transformations and exclusions.
Control
Checks a search, decoder or scorer using synthetic inputs or reference text; it does not identify the cipher of an unsolved page.
Alphabet-wide analysis
Studies a declared mathematical mapping over the alphabet, rather than decoding an unsolved page.
Input audit
Checks extraction or input coverage against a declared reference; it does not establish a decryption.
Historical reproduction
Replays a documented historical step with specified source material and conventions.
Method replay
Reconstructs a supplied proposed method and its choices; reproduction is not independent confirmation of a reading.
These describe the role of a record, not its outcome or confidence. A control linked to a method is not a test of that method on every unsolved page.

From a family to a test
For example, “try spirals” names a route family. A test must also fix the pages, grid dimensions, padding, starting cell, directions, any added substitution and the criterion for success. Changing one of these choices creates a variant; changing several may create a new experiment family.
