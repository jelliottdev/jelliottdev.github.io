Rune indices and prime values

The same rune has an alphabet position and a separate Gematria Primus prime value.

Two mappings, two jobs
The alphabet has 29 symbols. Its zero-based indices run from 0 to 28. Its prime values are the first 29 primes, from 2 to 109. F has index 0 and prime value 2; U has index 1 and prime value 3; TH has index 2 and prime value 5.
Known substitution ciphers use indices modulo 29. A Gematria Primus sum adds the prime values. Replacing one operation with the other changes the hypothesis.
Count symbols, not Latin characters
TH, EO, NG and other multi-letter labels each denote a single rune. U/V and C/K renderings also require the stated convention. Work from runes when computing sums and offsets.
Reproduce a small example
For F–U–TH, the indices are [0, 1, 2] and the prime sum is 2 + 3 + 5 = 10. Atbash transforms the indices to [28, 27, 26]; it does not reverse the digits of the sum.
