Known plaintext and crib searches

Use a declared plaintext hypothesis to constrain a defined cipher model.

A crib is a proposed plaintext fragment at a declared position. It constrains a cipher only through a specific model. In unrestricted additive decoding, any equal-length plaintext implies a key; computing that key does not independently support the proposed sentence.

Major variants

Variants include exact cribs, partial patterns, unknown-offset cribs, dictionary hypotheses and known-reading prefixes. Searching more phrases and positions creates more opportunities for an accidental match.

What a test must specify

Fix the cipher class, candidate phrases, rune encoding, offsets, allowed mismatches and key constraints. Distinguish a historical known plaintext from a guessed word. Record whether a crib was chosen before or after seeing a candidate.

How to evaluate a result

Use withheld text to test a key derived from a crib. Compare incompatible cribs and matched search controls. The existing nine-opening sweeps test those particular beginnings, not every possible plaintext under their cipher families.
