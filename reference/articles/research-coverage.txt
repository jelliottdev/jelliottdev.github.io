Research coverage: facts, attempts and next tests

What the archive already establishes, where its evidence is incomplete, and which bounded experiment series should come next.

The research archive currently contains 62 published reproducible records: 13 bounded solving or extraction tests, 1 proposed-method replay, 34 manuscript measurements, 7 controls, 4 source or input audits, 2 alphabet-wide analyses, and 1 historical reproduction. The method map has 54 families; 24 have no linked completed record. A missing link means not documented here, not “never tried”; a linked record covers only its stated inputs and bounds.

Record type matters as much as outcome. The prime-shift test applies a specified rule to unsolved pages. The doublet measurement describes a ciphertext feature. The tree-source audit checks artwork provenance. Only the first is a bounded attempt to use a candidate solving operation; none is a complete LP2 solve. Have we tried it? separates these roles when searching.


Start with the observation index when a count, pattern or visual feature prompts the idea. Use Have we tried it? to check a specific proposal against exact scopes, or the method-family map when you are still defining a method. This page tracks the work needed to make these routes a reliable account of what is known and what has been tested.


What counts as known or tried?

	Kind	What a page must supply	What it does not imply

	Source fact	A pinned image, file, or text; a location; and a way to inspect or download it.	That the feature is an instruction or a cipher key.

	Reproduced construction	An exact rule, all inputs, and a check against every claimed output. The LP2-15 square is an example.	That the construction decodes another page.

	Reported attempt	The proposer’s precise method, original source, and enough parameters to understand what was attempted. If the files are missing, label it unverified.	That Liberpedia reran it or that the method family is exhausted.

	Reproducible research record	Frozen inputs, code, bounds, output files, a record type and a conclusion no broader than the checked question.	That a measurement, source audit or control attempted to decode an unsolved page; or that other variants have the same result.


A failed-to-read output is not automatically a negative result. The experiment must say how success was measured and whether a known solved page passes the same pipeline. For large searches, record the number of variants and compare the best apparent match with a suitable control; otherwise a short English-looking fragment is easy to find by chance. See search space and bounds and the reproduction standard.

For results that do exclude a narrow model, use what has actually been ruled out?. Each entry names the assumption that makes the conclusion apply and the variants it leaves open. A general method-family page is not itself a negative result.


The factual spine that every test depends on

We already have page records, transcription checks, rune-value arithmetic, an illustrated index, and earlier puzzle history. The next audit must connect these into a per-claim ledger: original asset and hash; exact page and image region; transcription and disputed characters; calculation or reading; attribution; and confidence. A claim with only a derivative transcription should say so next to the claim, not only in a general disclaimer.

The most valuable reconciliation is at page boundaries and ambiguous marks. A change to a separator, rune, or line break can change word lengths, cribs, counts and key alignment. Build a contestable-locations register with the original crop, alternate readings, and downstream tests affected. Keep archived transcription, photographic observation, and proposed correction in separate fields. The current checking guide explains the distinction, but the register is not yet exhaustive.

Historical source chains need the same treatment. For each 2012–2014 book code, keep the edition or exact text, the coordinate convention, and a replay that yields the archived output. For artwork, distinguish a visually similar source from a verified pixel or compositing relationship. The LP2-55 source alignment and LP2-32/2016 comparisons now measure three related treatments; their exact compositing recipes remain open. For extracted files and later messages, identify the carrier, extraction command, original bytes, and authentication status. These are facts and provenance tasks before they are decryption experiments. Start with book ciphers, the cover, hidden files, and later messages.

Current gaps with the greatest downstream effect

	Gap	Why it matters	Next bounded piece of work

	Unresolved image glyphs	Five archived-versus-wiki grid cells alter 20 bits in a declared byte rendering and 16 prime-path suffix strings, although the prime counts survive. Other bare I/l stems have not been reviewed as a complete set.	Register every visually ambiguous cell with both original JPEG copies, its pixel location, alternate readings and the exact derived outputs it could change. Keep uncertainty in the dataset instead of silently choosing a glyph.

	Page-boundary and separator decisions	Rune-only agreement between two text sources does not settle word joining, punctuation or key-clock resets. A test may use these marks even when its rune frequency is unchanged.	Make a page-by-page contestable-location register, then rerun one fixed word-edge or reset experiment with one change at a time.

	Unexercised solving families	Twenty-four method families have no linked completed record. The largest useful candidates include running keys, homophonic/fractionated mappings, null removal and moving alphabets; an index page alone is not evidence they have been tried.	Choose one small, fixed parameter set per family, require a solved-page recovery control, and state what remains outside the bound.

	Search-pattern controls	The new prime-path shuffle test makes one retrospective pattern less compelling: three or more values are absent in about 15% of either control set. Other attractive patterns still need controls matched to the actual search procedure.	For each proposed pattern, freeze the selection rule and full search budget before comparing the same statistic with appropriate controls; preserve every candidate, not only the best-looking one.



The next experiment series

These are proposed series, not completed results. Each should have a baseline and linked variants rather than one page per tiny parameter change. The order favors tests that resolve common claims, validate the input data, or enable several later tests.

	Series	First bounded comparison	Controls and variants to preserve

	Transcription sensitivity	The baseline audit now compares the eight stored reviewed layers. Next, specify a punctuation-driven key clock or newly reviewed ambiguous mark and recompute its result.	Archive baseline; one change at a time; report which conclusions move. Do not combine uncertain edits into one preferred text.

	Running keys and cited works	Freeze a candidate source text and edition, alignment, rune mapping, clock and passage before scoring a chosen LP2 section.	Known LP1 reading; offset searches; unrelated texts of matched length; held-out passage. A thematic resemblance alone is not a key.

	LP2-49–51 table use	Compare explicit row, column, page and 256-cell traversal rules against the preserved case-sensitive table.	Separate base-60 decoding from a claimed route; record all tested orders, byte assumptions and stopping rules. Existing prime-path tests cover narrower routes.

	LP2-54–55 interruptions	Specify whether selected F runes split a stream, advance a clock, reset state or are skipped, then compare IoC and decoded controls.	Preserve the current skip and reset variants; add matched random interrupter positions and other segment rules.

	Nulls and doublet removal	Predeclare a small deletion or insertion rule and measure doublet reduction across LP2-00–55.	Known readings, preserved word boundaries, matched random deletion and count of searched rules. Uniform additive keys answer a different question.

	Moving alphabets	Fix a 29-rune disk, stepping rule and initial setting; test a single section before changing any rule.	Known-reading recovery, nearby settings, random ciphertext and held-out lines. A plausible sentence found after manual adjustments is exploratory.

	Artwork as a selector	Register exact dots, compass centers or marked paths on original images; convert one specified geometric rule to rune positions.	Pixel-scale uncertainty, alternate starting points, mirrored artwork and untouched pages. The current shared-radii result excludes only one fixed construction.

	Alternative readings and languages	State a rune-to-phoneme or symbol mapping and compare it to aligned same-content corpus controls.	Language scorer controls, tokenization, corpus provenance and held-out text. The eight-language comparison is a conditional fixed-substitution test, not a language identification.

	Homophonic and fractionated mappings	Constrain symbol multiplicity, coordinates and separators in advance, then evaluate a complete passage.	Known solved pages, random mappings with the same flexibility, and an explicit parameter count. Link variants to fractionation rather than declaring a family tested from one configuration.

	Layered pipelines	Freeze two operations and their order before reading the output, including exactly where page or word boundaries reset state.	Reverse order, each operation alone, shuffled controls and a holdout. Record the full search budget: flexible pipelines can fit short cribs by chance.



How a series becomes a published record

First preserve the original claim and distinguish its proposed rule from any observed output. Write a plan naming the input files, representation, parameter set, comparison statistic, controls, and exclusion criteria. Run it unchanged on a known-reading control, then on the target pages. Save code, results, plots and hashes. Publish the baseline and each material variant in one series, with a brief conclusion and a direct link back to the relevant page features. If the plan cannot be bounded yet, keep it in the family guide as an open method rather than presenting it as “tried.”

This register is an editorial snapshot, not a claim that unlisted approaches have never been explored. The published counts are regenerated from the current registry. Promote independently checkable work to a research record with its purpose labeled; only a bounded test of an explicit solving operation belongs in the solving-test group.
