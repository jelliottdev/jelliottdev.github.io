Fixed alphabet substitutions

Replace each rune consistently with one other symbol.

One fixed mapping replaces each input symbol throughout the chosen text. Caesar shifts and Atbash are small special cases; a general substitution can have many more choices. On a 29-symbol alphabet, an affine map uses y = ax + b modulo 29 with nonzero a. A permutation preserves symbol counts up to relabeling and preserves the pattern of equal symbols.

Major variants

Variants include Caesar, Atbash, affine maps, arbitrary alphabet permutations and keyed alphabets. A different map for each position belongs to a polyalphabetic or state-dependent family instead.

What a test must specify

Fix the alphabet order, direction of the map, treatment of non-rune marks, text span and permitted mapping class. A search over affine maps is not a search over all substitutions.

How to evaluate a result

Use synthetic encodings with known keys to test recovery. Check preserved equality patterns before language scoring. Evaluate full candidates and untouched text, rather than selecting isolated plausible words.
