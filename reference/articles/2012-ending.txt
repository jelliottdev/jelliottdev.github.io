2012: the Valēte closing message

Inspect the final Reddit JPEG, reproduce its OutGuess extraction and verify the signed message.

The last selected Mabinogion title was posted on 6 February 2012 at 18:02:15 UTC. It ends partway through a sentence. Five hours and 25 minutes later, the same account published “Valēte!”, linking a JPEG that states the search had found the people it sought. The surviving Reddit record supplies the post time, 23:27:05 UTC, and the image URL. We preserve a small source-record snapshot of those fields. It is not a screenshot of the original post. This gives the long Reddit title stream a dated endpoint without explaining why it continued after the phone-number book code.

What the image says

The visible message says the organizers had found the individuals they sought and that their month-long journey had ended “for now.” It thanks participants, says there will be other opportunities and ends with a postscript containing 131 digits across three displayed lines. The audit record preserves those digits exactly. Their presence is an observation, not a demonstrated solution to another puzzle.


What is in the JPEG

OutGuess retrieved 1,398 bytes from this saved JPEG, reporting seed 130. The extracted payload contains the closing wording and postscript in a clear-signed OpenPGP message. In a fresh run, the retrieved bytes matched the saved payload byte for byte. GPG verified its signature against the preserved 3301 public key, fingerprint 6D854CD7933322A601C3286D181F01E57A35090F. Verification establishes consistency with that key; the key is not independently certified to a real-world person or organization.


Reproduce the checks


Download the JPEG, payload and public key, or extract the reference-data bundle.

Run python scripts/replay_2012_valete.py to check the three saved hashes and the postscript length. This default check does not execute OutGuess or GPG.

For a fresh extraction and signature check, run python scripts/replay_2012_valete.py --outguess /path/to/outguess --gpg /path/to/gpg --output valete-check.json. The script uses a temporary isolated GPG keyring and compares fresh payload bytes with the saved payload. Download the script.


The published run report records the image and payload hashes, the OutGuess executable hash, the successful fresh byte comparison and GPG’s verified fingerprint. It does not claim a meaning for the postscript or a reason for the preceding 109 unused story lines.
