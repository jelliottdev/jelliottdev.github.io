Referenced works and possible influences

Find documented book sources, cover artwork identifications, and literary parallels with their evidence and limits.

Outside works enter this research in several ways. A text used for a book cipher, an identifiable fragment of a painting, and a sentence resembling another author’s words are different kinds of connection. This index explains what has been documented and takes you to the passages, images, and reproduction details.

What makes a source-image comparison useful?

Begin with the candidate’s original publication or collection record, not just a reposted crop. Record the artist or photographer where known, title, date, edition or object identifier, and the exact reproduction being compared. A later upload date does not necessarily date the underlying artwork.

Show the manuscript crop and candidate side by side, retaining enough surrounding detail to check the match. Identify corresponding features: for a wing, branching veins and enclosed cells; for a figure, the pose, hand, feet and internal contours. Record any reflection, rotation, crop, scaling or thresholding used to align them. If several transformations were tried, retain that search history rather than presenting the best overlay as if it were the only comparison.

Stroke weight, soft edges or a resemblance to a filtered photograph can suggest a production method, but do not establish it. Keep three questions separate: whether a composition matches, which reproduction was used, and how it was transformed. An apparent match also does not by itself identify the manuscript’s authors or supply a cipher key.

For comparisons within the manuscript, the cuneiform study provides an example of fixed-window pixel comparison; the image-region guide explains coordinates and derivatives. Exact equality after decoding is a different claim from visual similarity. Neither should be reported without naming the compared files and regions.

Texts and literary parallels

Emerson: Self-Reliance

Connection: a documented book-cipher source in the earlier 2014 route.

Inspect: the source PDF, the paragraph, sentence, word, and letter counting convention, and the reproduced historical address.

Blake: The Marriage of Heaven and Hell

Connection: a close verbal parallel with the Welcome’s statement about life being holy. Other passages support thematic comparison.

Inspect: the actual wording and its context in both works, including the different uses of circumference.

Carroll: Through the Looking-Glass

Connection: a possible association with the instruction to do four unreasonable things.

Inspect: chapter V’s White Queen dialogue. Compare doing with believing, four with six, and a daily practice with the recollection about breakfast.


Emerson’s role in the earlier route does not prove that every later statement about selfhood comes from Emerson. Likewise, the Carroll comparison is not a quotation match. The linked articles preserve these limits alongside the evidence.

Terms and traditions are another kind of reference

The manuscript explicitly labels two dialogues as koans. That establishes the label in the known reading; it does not supply a traditional case number, teacher, lineage, or source collection. The koan guide explains the genre and follows the dialogues before considering outside attribution.

Similarly, instar is a biological term used in the Welcome and final parable. Its definition helps explain the analogy, but it does not identify a source book or prove that every insect drawing depicts the same species. Philosophical themes follows connections internal to the manuscript.

The self and identity comparison shows how to read two works together while retaining their differences: Emerson’s affirmative account of self-trust is compared with the first koan’s unfinished answer.

English Qaballa: order and value
Connection: a proposed comparison with an outside alphabet-assignment system. The guide identifies the Crowley passage, reproduces the step-eleven table, and explains why a 26-letter construction cannot simply be treated as a 29-rune cipher. No manuscript quotation match or decoding result is established.

How to add a useful comparison

Locate both objects. Give the manuscript ID and the outside work’s edition, chapter, plate, or collection record.
Show the correspondence. Preserve exact wording or specify the matching visual features. Record cropping, rotation, or reflection.
Record differences. A shared subject is less specific than the same arrangement of details or a close verbal match.
State the claim narrowly. Distinguish a useful comparison from an identified source and from a proposed cipher input.

If the comparison suggests a key, use the reproduction guide to define the operation and scope. A bibliography entry does not establish that a technique has been tried; that belongs in documented experiment records.

Hakuin: The Cicada’s Wing
A traceable English passage about emergence and harmful assistance, compared with LP2-57. The guide corrects a copied recipient name, supplies edition details, and separates the literary parallel from the detached wing’s unidentified source.
