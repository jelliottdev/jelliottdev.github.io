Spirals and branching borders

Compare the curled borders on LP2-03–07 with the branching designs on LP2-40–55, and plan a measurable geometric comparison.

Several groups of Liber Primus pages have curled drawings beside the text. The community calls LP2-03–07 “Spirals,” LP2-40–53 “Spiral Branches,” and LP2-54–55 “Spiral & Dead Tree.” These names make the images easier to find. Comparing their actual linework is more useful than treating every curl as the same symbol.
The curled borders on LP2-03–07
On LP2-03, paired curves frame the left and right margins of the runic text. Each side has a tighter upper curl and a longer lower curve. LP2-07 retains this border arrangement, while its lower text ends above a red line and five large black dots.LP2-03 · ReadLP2-07 · Read
The dots are a separate feature from the small groups of specks beside the upper curls. See dots and punctuation for examples of why position, size, and arrangement should be recorded separately.

The branching borders on LP2-40–55
LP2-40 has curls joined to branching, root-like forms on both sides of the text. LP2-53 shows a winged insect below its red closing passage. The next pair, LP2-54–55, continues the branching border style; LP2-55 also contains a pale tree-shaped drawing above a winged insect.LP2-40 · ReadLP2-53 · ReadLP2-54 · ReadLP2-55 · Read
Use the illustrated index to inspect the intervening LP2-40–53 pages. The branches and trees article compares these forms with the narrow branching drawing on LP2-08–14.
A useful comparison starts with a question
	Question	What to record	What the result would establish
	Do two pages reuse the same border?	Original-file hashes, crop rectangles, alignment, and pixel differences	Agreement under the stated alignment and tolerance
	Are the two margins mirror images?	A defined reflection axis and corresponding points on both drawings	How closely the selected regions match after reflection
	Does a curl follow a particular spiral?	A centre, sampled points, coordinate scale, fitted parameters, and residuals	The quality of that geometric fit, compared with alternatives
For example, a logarithmic spiral has r = a exp(bθ), while an Archimedean spiral has r = a + bθ. Here r is distance from the chosen centre and θ is angle. Merely seeing a curl does not select either model. The chosen centre and the thickness of the drawn line can strongly affect a fit.
Before connecting geometry to a cipher, state how a measured result would change the decoding operation. “The borders look symmetric” and “read alternate lines backwards” are separate claims; the first does not supply the second without an explicit rule.
Preparing a reproducible image comparison
Choose the pages and the feature before fitting anything. Include a comparison page with a visibly different border.
Download the original files and verify their SHA-256 hashes.
Record rectangles using original-image coordinates. Keep the original pixels and any transformed comparison image separately.
Document scaling, rotation, reflection, thresholding, and the score used to compare the shapes.
Publish the script and output together, including mismatches. A visually plausible overlay alone is not enough to reproduce the comparison.
This article provides an inspection route and a comparison method. It does not report a completed geometric fit or establish a cipher role for the borders.
