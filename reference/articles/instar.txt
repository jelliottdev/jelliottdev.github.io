Instars, cicadas and emergence

The biological term, the linked Welcome and Parable passages, and the image beneath LP2-57.

An instar is a developmental stage between molts. Liber Primus uses the term in the Welcome and again in the final parable, connecting inward movement, shedding and emergence. Understanding the biological term makes the comparison more precise without turning the passage into an automatic cipher instruction.

The biological term

The Amateur Entomologists' Society glossary defines an instar as an arthropod's developmental stage between molts. The first instar follows hatching; successive molts lead to later instars. The term applies to insects with both complete and incomplete metamorphosis. It is not a synonym for a cocoon, and it does not name one particular insect species.

In cicadas, a nymph lives underground before emerging and undergoing the final molt into a winged adult. Purdue Extension's account of periodical cicadas describes nymphs leaving the soil, climbing a support and shedding their last shell. Its 13- and 17-year examples concern periodical cicadas; the word “instar” does not itself imply either duration.

Thirteen or seventeen years is not an instar count

The numbers 13 and 17 refer to the year-based life cycles of periodical cicadas, not to thirteen or seventeen developmental stages. An instar describes a stage between molts; a life-cycle duration describes elapsed time. Purdue Entomology’s cicada account distinguishes periodical and annual cicadas and describes the final emergence and molt. Its references to a particular spring and Brood X concern that emergence event, not a current-year forecast.

	Statement	What it identifies

	A 17-year periodical cicada	A life-cycle duration, not the number of times the animal molts.

	An instar	A developmental stage, not a fixed number of years.

	An exuvia	The discarded covering after a molt, not the stage or its duration.


Both 13 and 17 are prime integers. That provides a mathematical association with primality, but the known instar passages do not specify either number as a key. The biological fact, the arithmetic property and a proposed cipher operation are three separate claims.

For example, choosing a repeating key of length 13 would still leave its thirteen values unspecified. Choosing a constant shift of 13 would be a different operation; treating 13 as a prime ordinal would select the 13th prime, 41, rather than the value 13. State which meaning is intended before testing. See prime values and ordinal positions and bounded experiment specifications.

Where the manuscript uses it

Keep three things separate while reading: a stage of development, the act of shedding, and the material left behind. The manuscript’s metaphor draws on this general setting, but these biological terms do not name the same thing.

	Passage	Contextual English	Surrounding idea

	LP1-04, Welcome	LIKE THE INSTAR, IT IS ONLY THROUGH GOING WITHIN THAT WE MAY EMERGE	The pilgrimage shapes the self and reality; inward travel leads outward.

	LP2-57, Parable	LIKE THE INSTAR TUNNELING TO THE SURFACE	Shedding circumferences and finding divinity within precede emergence.


These are contextual English renderings. LP1-04's literal labels include LICE-THE-INSTAR and GONG-WITHIN; LP2-57 has LICE-THE-INSTAR-TUNNELNG-TO-THE-SURFACE after joining its line wrap. Keep these literal forms when checking runes or numbers. The contextual wording is not a replacement transcription. See the full Welcome, the full parable and reading conventions.

How the biological terms fit together

Instars are stages; molts separate them
Hatching: the first instar begins.
First instar: a stage of development before the next molt.
Molt: the old outer covering is shed.
Second instar: the next stage begins; later molts separate later instars.
This is a general sequence, not a count of cicada instars or a diagram of the manuscript’s cipher.

Stage, process, and remains

	Term	What it describes	Why the distinction matters here

	Instar	A developmental stage between molts	It is not the empty shell or a species name.

	Ecdysis	The shedding of the old exoskeleton during molting	It names an event rather than the entire intervening stage.

	Exuvia; plural exuviae	The discarded outer covering after a molt	The remains are distinct from the animal that has emerged.


The linked Amateur Entomologists’ Society definitions supply this terminology. Ecdysis and exuvia are explanatory vocabulary introduced here, not additional words decoded from the two cited manuscript passages. Finding a shell-like feature in a drawing would still require visual evidence; this glossary does not classify the LP2-57 figure as an exuvia.

For a different development pattern, compare mayflies and cicadas. The winged mayfly subimago is a specific stage; a community page-group name does not establish that a drawing depicts it.

What the repeated image contributes

The repeated term links two distant passages. The Welcome describes going within; the parable makes the movement toward the surface explicit. The parable also connects shedding with “circumferences,” a word used in The Loss of Divinity and the second koan. Together these support a literary interpretation of emergence through the removal of a limiting outer identity.

That interpretation is an analogy, not a biological definition. An insect's exoskeleton and the manuscript's circumference are not interchangeable technical terms. Nor is “going within” a complete description of cicada development. The text selects features of growth and emergence to make a comparison; it does not give an entomological account.

The image beneath the parable

The original LP2-57 image places a black, stippled, winged insect-like figure below the text. Its body runs vertically, with lateral wing shapes and scattered marks around it. Above it are the red heading and enlarged red initial among otherwise black runes. These are observations of the page, not an identification of a species or source illustration.

The adjacent emergence text makes an insect comparison relevant, but it cannot by itself establish what species the drawing represents. A visual attribution would need a matching source image or diagnostic features, with differences recorded. Do not use the surrounding prose as proof that every feature of the drawing is anatomically cicadan. Use the reader's enlarged original to inspect the marks rather than treating a small thumbnail as sufficient evidence.

Why the exact spelling still matters

LP2-57's TUNNELNG uses the multi-letter NG/ING rune convention. Inserting a separate I changes the prime sum even if the contextual English looks more familiar. The prime-sum guide reproduces the arithmetic: adding I contributes 31, changing the first body sentence's sum from 1259 to 1290. This is a numerical consequence of a specified alternate encoding, not proof that the author chose the original to achieve that sum.

Likewise, the biology of periodical cicadas does not justify selecting 13 or 17 as a key without a further rule. A test using those numbers needs explicit page scope, transformation, offsets and criteria. A thematic association supplies a candidate, not a result.
