Dots and punctuation

Find the five large dots on LP2-07, the separated dots on LP2-33 and LP2-39, and small punctuation clusters.

“Dots” can mean several visibly different things in Liber Primus. Large dots separated by wide spaces, small marks between runes, and compact multi-dot clusters should not be treated as one symbol just because a transcription uses similar punctuation for them.
Five large dots on LP2-07
LP2-07 has five large black dots beneath its red closing line. They form a loose arrangement rather than one straight row. Smaller specks also appear beside the upper spiral drawings, so “the dots on page 7” should specify which group is meant.LP2-07 · Read
See spirals and branching borders for the surrounding LP2-03–07 group.
Large separated dots
LP2-33 has three conspicuous black dots between two blocks of text. LP2-39 also has a separated row of three dots. These are useful starting points when someone remembers “the page with dots.” This pair is a set of illustrated examples, not an exhaustive dot inventory of all 75 pages.Three large separated dots between the text blocks on LP2-33. Small marks and compact clusters
The LP2-00–02 inventory records punctuation separately from rune shapes. Its measured regions include one-, four-, and thirteen-component groups. A “component” is a connected set of dark pixels under a specified threshold. It is not automatically one intentional dot: touching marks can merge, and tiny specks can be excluded.Published measurements for the opening pages

The printed mark and the exported character
In the archive notation, a comma represents a three-dot symbol; a period is a clause separator, and an ampersand is a paragraph separator. These are editorial conventions. They are not instructions to insert the prime value of a comma, reset a key, or skip a rune.
One exported character can stand for visually different marks. Before a punctuation-based experiment, identify the exact image regions and the precise sequence you are testing. Otherwise a result may describe the transcriber’s decisions rather than the printed manuscript.
