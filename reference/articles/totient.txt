Euler’s totient and prime-minus-one

Why φ(p) equals p − 1 for primes, and what that means for LP2-56.

Euler’s totient function, written φ(n), counts the integers from 1 through n that are relatively prime to n. Two integers are relatively prime when their greatest common divisor is 1. “Relatively prime” does not mean that both integers are themselves prime.

Small examples
For n = 8, the qualifying integers are 1, 3, 5, and 7, so φ(8) = 4. For a prime p, every integer from 1 through p − 1 is relatively prime to p. Therefore φ(p) = p − 1. In particular, φ(2), φ(3), φ(5), φ(7) are 1, 2, 4, 6.

Why the LP2-56 method has two names
The archived walkthrough describes LP2-56 using “phi(prime).” The prime-minus-one guide uses the equivalent expression p − 1. These are the same shifts when the input sequence consists of primes. Substituting φ(n) for consecutive integers n would produce a different sequence.

How to reproduce the identity
from math import gcd
def phi(n):
    return sum(gcd(k, n) == 1 for k in range(1, n + 1))
assert phi(8) == 4
assert [phi(p) for p in [2, 3, 5, 7]] == [1, 2, 4, 6]

What the identity does not determine
It does not specify the starting prime, direction, reset behavior, or copied rune positions. Those parameters remain part of the keystream convention.
