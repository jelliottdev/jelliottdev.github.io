Alternative alphabets and symbolic notations

Define a symbol inventory and an encoding rule before interpreting resemblance.

A proposed alternative notation maps visible features to symbols in another system. Dots, bars or gaps might suggest Morse or Braille, but resemblance alone does not determine segmentation, orientation or reading order.

Major variants

Possible subfamilies include binary feature coding, Morse-like durations, Braille cells, Bacon-style groups, musical notation and custom symbol inventories. Each needs its own explicit mapping and applicable source features.

What a test must specify

Define measured features, grouping, orientation, ordering, thresholds, symbol table and invalid-group handling. Keep original image measurements separate from their interpretation. State how missing or ambiguous marks are represented.

How to evaluate a result

Apply the mapping consistently to all eligible features and retain invalid outputs. Compare with controls that preserve relevant layout or feature counts. A few recognizable letters obtained by changing grouping rules are not a complete encoding.

Documented experiments
