Möbius and the crossed-loop drawing

Find the LP2-15–22 grouping, inspect the loop beneath the number panel, and distinguish the visual name from a geometric claim.

The name Möbius (also written Mobius or Moebius) is used for the LP2-15–22 section. The most recognizable detail is the crossed, two-lobed drawing beneath the numbers on LP2-15. Start there if you remember an infinity-shaped symbol or a number square.
[Image: LP2-15: the number panel, crossed loop, and runic text on the complete original sheet.]
LP2-15: the number panel, crossed loop, and runic text on the complete original sheet. Page record · Original JPEG
What the drawing shows
The two lobes cross, but their outlines have visible openings. The recorded inspection describes an open-loop drawing. “Infinity sign” is a useful way to find it; it does not establish an exact Unicode symbol or a number encoded by that symbol.
A Möbius strip is a surface made by joining the ends of a strip with a half-twist. An infinity sign is a two-dimensional figure-eight shape. Those are different objects: a crossed outline alone cannot demonstrate that a surface has a half-twist. A proposed Möbius interpretation needs to specify how the page’s lines, crossings, and gaps encode that surface.
The name also belongs to the Möbius function μ(n), an arithmetic rule based on prime factors. Its outputs are −1, 0 and 1. The linked guide explains that meaning separately; the drawing and the word MOBIUS do not establish that this function is the intended cipher operation.

The text-only middle pages
LP2-17–21 each contain twelve rows of black runes within broad blank margins. The crossed-loop drawing on LP2-15 is not repeated on these sheets. Their page records now include regions you can open in the image inspector: 17, 18, 19, 20, and 21.
A spacing difference on LP2-21
On LP2-21, a wider gap separates the second and third rows. That is a layout observation, not a decoded paragraph break. If you test a rule that resets at this gap, record the reset as an assumption and compare its result with the same rule without the reset.

Punctuation is a separate layer
Small dots and compact dotted clusters occur among the runes and sometimes at the start of a row. Examples include the start of row three on LP2-19 and row seven on LP2-20. The dots guide explains why visual marks and transcription punctuation need to be distinguished. For rune-only statistics such as index of coincidence, punctuation is excluded from the symbol counts. A study of spacing or separators needs to preserve that information separately; excluding it from one calculation does not show that it has no role.
The closing page: crossbars and a reclining figure
LP2-22 looks quite different from the opening number panel. Six rows of black runes end in a small red dotted mark. Under the text sits a dark horizontal drawing with three thick crossbars; farther down, a reclining human figure extends across the bottom edge, feet to the left and head to the right.
[Image: Lower portion of LP2-22: the dark three-crossbar drawing above a reclining human figure.]
Detail of the original LP2-22 image, from below the text to the bottom edge. Read the full page · Download the original JPEG
The figure is a useful feature to search for and compare, but an identification needs more than a resemblance. Its posture alone cannot establish a death scene, a named person, or a borrowed composition. The page record has separate inspection regions for the red mark, the crossbar drawing, and the figure.
Reclining figures also appear along the bottoms of LP1-14–16. On LP1-14, the figure sits beneath the second koan on LP1-14. On all three LP1 sheets the head is on the left and the feet on the right, opposite to LP2-22. This gives a concrete comparison across the two releases, without establishing that the images are exact reflections or that their accompanying texts share a cipher.

Even within this grouping, layouts vary. LP2-16, immediately after the panel page, has twelve rows of black text and no separate illustration. Treat the community section name as a way to locate these sheets; whether their text shares a key or transformation remains a separate question.
Following the numerical details
The panel contains sixteen numbers; 3299 is red. The panel article gives every value, row and column totals, and a reproducible calculation. For an idea involving prime positions or reversed digits, use prime indices and emirps before assigning those operations to the drawing.
There is also a literal MOBIUS in the known Some Wisdom reading. That textual occurrence is a separate piece of evidence. A shared name is a reason to compare the pages, not proof that one supplies a key for the other.
