2014 square submission: three pages and a signed reply

Preserve the original LP1-14–16 page copies, distinguish the lower-resolution first file, and verify the signed pointer to the next onion.

The archived account places three more Liber Primus pages after the magic-square submission form. It labels the files 107.jpg, 167.jpg and 229.jpg. They correspond to LP1-14 through LP1-16: the second koan and the following instruction. A separate preserved, signed reply points to the next onion address.

This is a source trail, not a reconstruction of a particular participant's submission. The archived stage account says the pages followed submission; the saved pages and reply let us check their contents and signatures, but do not supply a server transaction log proving who submitted what.

What the pages contain

LP1-14 and LP1-15 form one continuous second-koan reading. The documented Vigenère key is FIRFUMFERENFE; key alignment continues across the page boundary and skips specified ᚠ instances. A partial fit on one line would not verify that control. The koan guide explains the dialogue in ordinary language while keeping the literal rune output separate. LP1-16 is a different, unencrypted instruction with a number square. The magic-square guide checks its sums. These are known readings, not proposed solutions to LP2.

The signed follow-up

The preserved message.txt.asc says “Your enlightenment awaits you” and gives ky2khlqdf7qdznac.onion. Its signature verifies against the archived Cicada public key with fingerprint 6D854CD7933322A601C3286D181F01E57A35090F. The archive account dates the follow-up to May 2014 and quotes example HTTP requests to a participant's hidden service. Those request lines are an archived report; we have not independently authenticated the original server logs. The signed text establishes the message bytes and their signature under the preserved key, not a sender's real-world identity or a particular recipient.

Reproduce and download

Download reference-data.zip and original-images.zip, extracting both into the same folder. With Python, Pillow and GnuPG installed, run python scripts/replay_2014_square_submission.py --check. The script checks every preserved source against its pinned manifest, compares the three JPEGs to the hosted page files, and verifies the signed reply in an isolated keyring. The report includes the limits as well as the matches. All cited source copies are hosted here; the original archive revision remains linked for provenance.
