Disputed runes and word breaks: location register

Find the exact positions of published reviewed-text edits and inspect rune-to-rune LP2 page edges without treating them as confirmed word joins.

This register puts the published transcription edits and possible LP2 page joins in one place. It records what is in the site’s data, not every rune or mark that might be disputed. Use each location to inspect the original JPEG before deciding which reading an experiment should use.

Recorded reviewed-layer edits

Eight pages have separate reviewed-text layers. They contain eleven recorded edits: one rune-order change and ten changes to non-rune notation. An exact text offset helps find a mark; it is not an image-coordinate measurement or a fresh visual verification. The page record preserves the correction note and the original-image hash. See how the archived and reviewed layers differ.

Possible joins across LP2 page breaks
At 32 of the 55 adjacent boundaries from LP2-00 through LP2-55, a rune is the last retained character on one page and a rune is the first on the next in the archived text. That observation does not say a word crosses the boundary. The images have not been checked for this inventory, and punctuation or section structure may change the interpretation. The complete 55-boundary inventory also contains the other 23 edges.
For a word-edge experiment, inspect both original images, record the treatment of the join, and rerun the calculation under both interpretations if uncertain.
