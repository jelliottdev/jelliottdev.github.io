Running keys from external texts

Convert a specified external text into a stream of key symbols.

A running-key cipher consumes a stream derived from an external text rather than repeatedly cycling a short keyword. The reference work, its edition and conversion into rune indices are all part of the key specification. This differs from a book cipher whose coordinates directly select letters.

Major variants

Variants include continuous additive keys, passage restarts, page-dependent offsets, alternating sources and text-derived numerical streams. Referenced philosophical works can motivate candidates, but thematic similarity does not validate their decoding output.

What a test must specify

Freeze source files and hashes, normalization, rune segmentation, starting offsets, key direction and restart rules. State whether headings, punctuation and footnotes contribute symbols. Bound both the book list and the offsets.

How to evaluate a result

Use an encoder with a known source passage as a positive control and unrelated texts as comparisons. A source or offset selected on an opening should predict an untouched continuation. Keep failed candidate books in the search record.
