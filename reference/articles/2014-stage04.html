2014 server captures: LP1-03 through LP1-05

Replay the preserved hexadecimal responses, recover three exact manuscript JPEGs, and extract the 1033 matrix embedded in LP1-05.

On this page
  • Two captures at the same address
  • The server-status capture and its second layer
  • Replay and verify

Three more first-release manuscript pages can be traced to archived 2014 server captures. A later index.html.1 response yields LP1-03 and LP1-04. A saved server-status page yields LP1-05. The recovered JPEGs match the originals hosted here byte for byte. These are saved historical files, not responses from a service that is still running.

This step follows the three-image chain that recovered LP1-00 through LP1-02. It documents how the next images were preserved and recovered; it is not a proposed decryption of LP2.

Continue with the archived fourth-onion response to recover the four pages of the first koan, LP1-06 through LP1-09.

Two captures at the same address

The archive keeps an initial index capture marked <!--1033-->. Its hexadecimal body decodes to 256 bytes. A later index capture is marked <!--3301--> and decodes to 2,116,096 bytes. The smaller capture is a separate observation; it is not the beginning of the larger decoded stream. The saved files do not establish when or why the response changed.

Decode the later body from hexadecimal. Its first 892,759 bytes are exactly the LP1-03 JPEG. Reverse the entire decoded byte stream; its first 1,223,337 bytes are exactly the LP1-04 JPEG. The two lengths add to the full stream length. Do not reverse each line of hexadecimal text or reverse just the second image segment: those are different operations.

The server-status capture and its second layer

A separate saved server-status response contains a single block of hexadecimal text between <pre> tags. Hex-decoding that block gives the 673,066-byte LP1-05 JPEG exactly. Here “reverse hexdump” in the archived notes means converting printed hexadecimal back to bytes; it does not mean reversing the JPEG’s byte order.

There is another layer inside that JPEG. Take 360 bytes beginning at zero-based offset 336,353, reverse those bytes, then hex-decode them. The result is the 179-byte archived matrix text:

272 138 341 131 151
366 199 130 320  18
226 245  91 245 226
 18 320 130 199 366
151 131 341 138 272

Each of its five rows, five columns and two main diagonals totals 1033. It is the same numerical grid obtained by converting the rune words printed on LP1-05 to Gematria Primus prime sums. The embedded copy supports that historical construction; it does not, by itself, tell us how to read an unsolved page.

Replay and verify

Download reference-data.zip and original-images.zip, extracting both into the same folder. Run python scripts/replay_2014_stage04.py --check from that folder. The script checks the archived source hashes, performs both image recoveries, compares all three outputs with the hosted originals, extracts the matrix, and checks all twelve sums. Add --extract-dir recovered-pages to save the three verified JPEGs without replacing existing files. The replay report gives every byte count and output hash; the manifest pins the source files to a specific archive revision.

The captures and matrix are hosted here so their availability does not depend on the external archive. For provenance, compare the pinned stage04 directory. The page-image equality is a direct byte comparison; it does not establish the timing of the live service or a unique purpose for every embedded byte.
