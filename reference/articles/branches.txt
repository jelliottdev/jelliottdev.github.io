Branches and trees

Locate the narrow branching drawings on LP2-08–14 and the pale tree on LP2-55; distinguish tips, junctions, and segments before counting.

Branching forms occur in both releases, but they are not one established symbol system. This guide compares the Welcome’s leafy frame, the alternating margin drawing on LP2-08–14, later curled borders, and the central tree on LP2-32 and LP2-55. The tree source comparison links that last image to a reflected Wikimedia photograph and records measured alignments; its exact processing steps remain unknown. A source-image match does not supply a reading rule for the surrounding runes.
The leafy border on the Welcome pages
LP1-03 and LP1-04 place their text inside thick curving branches, with dense leaf shapes above and more open loops along the sides. Branches turn inward beneath the text. The broad arrangement is shared across the two sheets; describing that resemblance is not a pixel-level claim that every line is identical.

The coordinates refer to the original 2400 × 3600 images, with the origin at the upper-left. Each window contains 1,375,000 RGB pixels, including background. The windows were chosen after inspecting the artwork and are not claimed to isolate every branch tip.

Exact equality supports reuse of the same decoded margin image within each three-page group. It does not make the entire pages identical, establish a reflection or translation between the left and right groups, or prove that LP2-08 came from different source artwork. Different processing or placement can change pixels while retaining the same drawing. No cipher meaning follows from this comparison.

The report records all seven source hashes, crop hashes and all 21 pairwise byte comparisons. Extract the reference-data and original-images archives into the same folder, install Pillow, and run:

python scripts/branch_margin_reuse.py

The script recomputes the report without changing it. The saved result was produced with Pillow 11.3.0; record your decoder version if it differs. A mismatch is a reason to inspect decoder or input differences, not to overwrite the expected hashes. Compare the separate side-dot reuse study, which uses its own windows and page set.

Across the margins: translation without reflection

The right-side branch on LP2-09 and left-side branch on LP2-10 can be compared directly after a horizontal translation. This is a separate measurement from the exact within-side RGB reuse above. It compares thresholded dark-pixel masks rather than asserting identical JPEG pixels.

For LP2-09, take rectangle [1800, 600, 550, 2350]; for LP2-10, take [0, 600, 550, 2350]. Convert to Pillow grayscale and mark pixels below 128. Within those crop coordinates, moving the right-hand mask 16 pixels right, with no vertical shift or reflection, gives 98.49% Dice overlap. In full-page coordinates this maps x_left = x_right − 1784 and leaves y unchanged.

Dice overlap is 2 × intersection / (source dark pixels + target dark pixels). Here the counts are 44,806 source pixels, 44,805 target pixels and 44,129 shared pixels. White background does not inflate this score. The result describes the selected masks, not a probability that two drawings share a source.

	Orientation tested	Best overlap in the stated range
	Same orientation	98.49%
	Horizontal reflection	18.44%
	Vertical reflection	9.78%
	Both reflections (180° turn)	15.95%

Each orientation was tested at 3,417 integer translations: x shifts −100 through 100 and y shifts −8 through 8, inclusive, within the crop coordinates. The crop and search bounds were chosen after viewing the originals; this is an exploratory comparison, not a preregistered significance test. No scaling or flexible warping was searched. The reflected results are only maxima within these bounds.

[Image: Top of the aligned branch masks: shared dark pixels dominate, with small blue and orange differences along the edges.]
Measurement overlay, not an original page image. Dark pixels are shared; blue appears only in translated LP2-09, orange only in LP2-10. The preview shows the upper 800 pixels of the tested window. Select it for the full overlay.

The fixed same-orientation shift was then applied to all nine combinations of right-side pages 09/11/13 and left-side pages 10/12/14. Every pair gives the same score at each tested threshold: 97.84% below 64, 98.49% below 128, and 98.80% below 192. These similar pages are not independent evidence samples. LP2-08 is outside this alignment comparison.

The measurement supports translated reuse in the same orientation for these windows. It does not reconstruct the author’s processing steps, explain the remaining pixel differences, or support reversing the ciphertext on alternate pages.

Use the reference-data bundle with the original images extracted into the same directory. This script was checked with Pillow 11.3.0 and NumPy 2.5.4. It verifies the fixed-shift intersections independently with Pillow logical masks in addition to the NumPy calculation.

python -m pip install Pillow==11.3.0 numpy==2.5.4

What does “count the branches” mean?
A branch count sounds straightforward until two people trace the same drawing differently. One may count every visible line leaving a trunk; another may count terminal tips; a third may count each segment between junctions. Those are different measurements.
	Quantity	A possible explicit definition
	Tips	Endpoints of a traced line, excluding the boundary of the chosen crop
	Junctions	Locations where three or more traced segments meet
	Segments	Paths between tips or junctions, with no junction in their interior
	Main stem	A selected path identified by its endpoints and a stated selection rule
Under a graph representation, tips and junctions become vertices and the connecting segments become edges. A visually tree-like drawing need not yield a mathematical tree: a crossing may or may not be a connection, and a closed loop would change the graph. Publish an annotated tracing so another person can see those choices.
Handling the pale drawing on LP2-55
Increasing contrast can make a faint line easier to see, but it also changes the displayed pixels. Preserve the original file, label an enhanced image as a derivative, and record the operation. If a claim depends on a feature visible only after enhancement, show the corresponding unmodified region too.
For numerical work, choose the brightness threshold before counting and check how modest changes affect the result. A count that changes sharply with the threshold needs that sensitivity reported. The existing image-region guide explains thresholds used for other published measurements; those settings have not been validated as a segmentation method for this pale tree.
From appearance to interpretation
A botanical identification, a religious association, and a decoding instruction each require different evidence. A comparison with a named artwork should show the proposed source image and the matching features. A cipher proposal should specify how the drawing produces an ordered sequence or operation, rather than selecting a count because it happens to fit.
For wider interpretation, start with philosophical themes in the known readings. That article distinguishes what the text says from suggested connections. No particular symbolic meaning is assigned to these branching drawings here.

These four sheets each have twelve rows of black runes between tall curling stems. Look for the drooping narrow leaves, the root-like lower branches, and the groups of filled dots beside the stems. The page records provide original-image inspection regions: 41, 42, 43, and 44.

A related border arrangement also frames the alphanumeric grids on LP2-49–51. This gives a visual comparison across different text layouts. It does not by itself establish identical artwork files, a plant species, or a shared cipher.
Keep the border dots separate from marks within the text. LP2-42 has paired short vertical strokes near the right ends of rows three and six; LP2-43 has a pair near the end of row one. Those observations are recorded on the individual pages. Their resemblance to familiar punctuation is not enough to assign a grammatical role; the punctuation guide explains how to preserve visual distinctions in an analysis.
The runic pages around the grids
The same broad border arrangement continues on LP2-45, 46, 47, and 48. Each has twelve runic rows. LP2-49–51 then place alphanumeric arrays inside the borders, before LP2-52 returns to a full twelve-row runic block.
	Pages	Central content
	LP2-45–48	Twelve runic rows on each sheet
	LP2-49–51	Alphanumeric grids; runic text above the first and below the last
	LP2-52	Twelve runic rows
That sequence is useful when defining a test's scope. A proposal based on the grid entries should say whether it also uses the surrounding runes or border marks. The change in central content does not by itself demonstrate a new key, cipher, or instruction to reset an operation.
There are also smaller differences worth inspecting. LP2-47 ends its first row with a dotted cluster. On LP2-48, small dots precede the first runes of rows four and six. The page records locate these examples in the originals; neither their placement nor the shared border assigns them a decoded meaning.

Comparison: horizontal reflection brings the broad branch arrangement into correspondence. In the photograph the low, nearly horizontal limb extends left; in LP2-55 it extends right. The main fork above it and the taller upward branches also exchange sides. These are more specific comparison points than the shared subject of a leafless tree.

The photograph retains ground, distant vegetation, bark texture and fine twigs. LP2-55 isolates a pale outline with much less detail. The photograph is the source comparison for the shared tree, including the processed version on LP2-32 and the 2016 message. A bounded alignment experiment now compares the credited Commons original with LP2-55 in both orientations. The reflected crop fits more closely under its declared grayscale, scale and translation rule; it does not reconstruct the tracing or exact filter sequence.

How to test the source comparison

Preserve the photograph's original bytes and its file-page revision alongside the manuscript JPEG. Record the crop and horizontal reflection, then fit translation and scale using a small, stated set of major forks. Check additional branches that were not used to fit the alignment. If rotation, stretching or a more flexible warp is introduced, report it: more adjustable parameters make an apparent match easier to obtain.

Compare the aligned images before trying edge detection or thresholding. Record every processing setting and show the original beside each derivative. Similarity after one chosen filter would support that reconstruction; it would not show that the puzzle's author used that software or those exact settings. See recording image regions and search bounds.

The photograph's stated location identifies its subject. It does not establish where the puzzle authors lived, worked or obtained the file. For the file description used here, see the archived Commons revision.
