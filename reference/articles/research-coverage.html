Research coverage: facts, attempts and next tests

What the archive already establishes, where its evidence is incomplete, and which bounded experiment series should come next.

The research archive currently contains 95 published reproducible records: 20 bounded solving or extraction tests, 3 proposed-method replay, 43 manuscript measurements, 10 controls, 16 source or input audits, 2 alphabet-wide analyses, and 1 historical reproduction. The method map has 54 families; 18 have no linked completed record. A missing link means not documented here, not “never tried”; a linked record covers only its stated inputs and bounds.

Record type matters as much as outcome. The prime-shift test applies a specified rule to unsolved pages. The doublet measurement describes a ciphertext feature. The tree-source audit checks artwork provenance. Only the first is a bounded attempt to use a candidate solving operation; none is a complete LP2 solve. Have we tried it? separates these roles when searching.

What remains in the supplied-source intake: 14 reported ideas are not replayed, 24 are partly replayed, and 10 have related work but no exact replay. Of 15 additional supplied source files, 15 have been screened: 12 retain specific follow-ups, 3 yielded no further distinct claim in the supplied file, and 0 remain unscreened. Screening classifies the supplied extracts; it does not complete their replays. These groups count reports and files, not additional experiments: equivalent claims can share a test, while one report may need several fixed variants. The source-specific intake names the exact missing work.

Start with the observation index when a count, pattern or visual feature prompts the idea. Use Have we tried it? to check a specific proposal against exact scopes, or the method-family map when you are still defining a method. This page tracks the work needed to make these routes a reliable account of what is known and what has been tested.

The reported-attempt intake now names source-specific prior proposals separately from completed experiments. It states where our records overlap and what an exact replay still needs. This first set is deliberately incomplete: it prevents a documented report from being mistaken for a verified result while the wider source inventory is assembled.

A separate public-repository intake has identified a 172-entry research ledger in one repository; 48 entries are labelled negative by its author. Those labels have not been converted into Liberpedia results, and the entries have not been deduplicated against the 43 reports above. Three targeted source probes have exposed limits in the published experiments, including a red-initial image classification error. Neither 172 nor 48 is an additional count of verified or distinct experiments. The 327 GitHub search hits are candidates for inspection, not a census of tests.

What counts as known or tried?

KindWhat a page must supplyWhat it does not imply
Source factA pinned image, file, or text; a location; and a way to inspect or download it.That the feature is an instruction or a cipher key.
Reproduced constructionAn exact rule, all inputs, and a check against every claimed output. The LP2-15 square is an example.That the construction decodes another page.
Reported attemptThe proposer’s precise method, original source, and enough parameters to understand what was attempted. If the files are missing, label it unverified.That Liberpedia reran it or that the method family is exhausted.
Reproducible research recordFrozen inputs, code, bounds, output files, a record type and a conclusion no broader than the checked question.That a measurement, source audit or control attempted to decode an unsolved page; or that other variants have the same result.

A failed-to-read output is not automatically a negative result. The experiment must say how success was measured and whether a known solved page passes the same pipeline. For large searches, record the number of variants and compare the best apparent match with a suitable control; otherwise a short English-looking fragment is easy to find by chance. See search space and bounds and the reproduction standard.

For results that do exclude a narrow model, use what has actually been ruled out?. Each entry names the assumption that makes the conclusion apply and the variants it leaves open. A general method-family page is not itself a negative result.

The factual spine that every test depends on

We already have page records, transcription checks, rune-value arithmetic, an illustrated index, and earlier puzzle history. The next audit must connect these into a per-claim ledger: original asset and hash; exact page and image region; transcription and disputed characters; calculation or reading; attribution; and confidence. A claim with only a derivative transcription should say so next to the claim, not only in a general disclaimer.

The most valuable reconciliation is at page boundaries and ambiguous marks. A change to a separator, rune, or line break can change word lengths, cribs, counts and key alignment. Build a contestable-locations register with the original crop, alternate readings, and downstream tests affected. Keep archived transcription, photographic observation, and proposed correction in separate fields. The current checking guide explains the distinction, but the register is not yet exhaustive.

Historical source chains need the same treatment. For each 2012–2014 book code, keep the edition or exact text, the coordinate convention, and a replay that yields the archived output. For artwork, distinguish a visually similar source from a verified pixel or compositing relationship. The LP2-55 source alignment and LP2-32/2016 comparisons now measure three related treatments; their exact compositing recipes remain open. For extracted files and later messages, identify the carrier, extraction command, original bytes, and authentication status. These are facts and provenance tasks before they are decryption experiments. Start with book ciphers, the cover, hidden files, and later messages.

How a series becomes a published record

First preserve the original claim and distinguish its proposed rule from any observed output. Write a plan naming the input files, representation, parameter set, comparison statistic, controls, and exclusion criteria. Run it unchanged on a known-reading control, then on the target pages. Save code, results, plots and hashes. Publish the baseline and each material variant in one series, with a brief conclusion and a direct link back to the relevant page features. If the plan cannot be bounded yet, keep it in the family guide as an open method rather than presenting it as “tried.”

This register is an editorial snapshot, not a claim that unlisted approaches have never been explored. The published counts are regenerated from the current registry. Promote independently checkable work to a research record with its purpose labeled; only a bounded test of an explicit solving operation belongs in the solving-test group.
