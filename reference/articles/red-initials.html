Red initials in LP2: an image check

Where enlarged red runes actually appear, and why counting all red pixels misclassified two pages in a published experiment.

On this page
  • Where the large initials appear
  • What this changes
  • Check the images and calculation

Thirteen LP2 pages have at least one large red initial. A published image scan listed fifteen. The difference is visible on LP2-07 and LP2-53: both contain red text, but neither contains a large red initial. Counting every red pixel on the page makes these two look like initials to the scanner.

Where the large initials appear

00, 03, 06, 08, 15, 23, 27, 33, 39, 40, 54, 56 and 57. LP2-03 and LP2-33 each have two large red initials. On LP2-06, LP2-15 and LP2-39, a large initial begins a passage below earlier content. A sequence made from only the first rune of each page would miss some of what is actually printed.

What this changes

The published drop-cap analysis tested whether its fifteen selected pages formed an unusual sequence. Our check addresses an earlier step: whether those pages were correctly classified. It does not establish a hidden reading or show that a corrected thirteen-page sequence is statistically unusual. The sequence tests would need to be rerun with a rule for multiple and later initials.

Check the images and calculation

Open the complete original images for LP2-07 and LP2-53. The 58-page measurement table records the image hashes and both classifications. The source audit explains the exact rule, dependencies and limits; its script reruns the image measurement against the site's original JPEGs.
