Book ciphers: coordinates, editions and counting rules

How text lookups work and what to preserve so another reader can reproduce them.

A book cipher turns coordinates into text by looking them up in a shared source. The source can be an essay, poem or other document; it need not be a whole book. To reproduce a result, knowing the title is not enough. You also need the edition and the rules used to count its parts.

How a four-part lookup works

In a paragraph:sentence:word:letter scheme, 2:1:3:2 means: select paragraph 2, then its first sentence, then its third word, then the second letter of that word. This assumes one-based counting, where the first item is numbered 1.

For a small invented example, let paragraph 2 begin We study runes. The third word is runes, so the second letter is u. The coordinates identify nested locations; they are not numbers to add together.

The archived 2014 opening puzzle uses this kind of rule with Emerson’s Self-Reliance. That guide walks through its first coordinate against the referenced PDF and distinguishes that check from a full reproduction.

Decide what counts before extracting
Starting pointState whether titles, opening quotations and notes are included. Starting at the first prose paragraph can differ from starting at the first text on the page.
Paragraph boundariesPreserve actual paragraph divisions. A page or display line ending may interrupt a paragraph without creating a new one.
Sentence boundariesSpecify how punctuation and abbreviations are handled. Splitting blindly at every period can create false sentences.
Word and letter boundariesRecord how hyphens, apostrophes, punctuation and accented letters are treated. Counting characters in a raw file is not necessarily counting letters in the printed word.
Literal charactersA clue may print some digits or punctuation directly. Identify those separately instead of forcing every item through the coordinate rule.

What a reproducible trace should show

For each coordinate, record the selected paragraph, sentence, word and letter, plus the resulting output character. Include the source URL, a file checksum, and any transcription changes. A reader should be able to inspect one disagreeing character without rerunning an opaque decoder.

If a coordinate points beyond a sentence or word, report it as out of range. Wrapping around, skipping a heading or switching editions introduces another rule. Such a rule may be worth testing, but it must be stated rather than hidden inside a correction.

Using a possible literary reference

A recognizable quotation or artwork can suggest a source to investigate. It does not identify the indexing scheme. Before trying many passages and counting conventions, write down the source, coordinates, transformations and success criterion. Otherwise a promising fragment may simply be the best-looking result among many undocumented choices.

The experiment-writing guide explains how to preserve that scope. Use Have we tried it? to look for a documented test of the same technique and inputs, rather than treating any mention of the source as evidence that the test was performed.
