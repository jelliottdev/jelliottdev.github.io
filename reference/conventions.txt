Transcription guide

Numbering, symbols, and editorial conventions

Page identifiers

LP1 contains pages 00–16; LP2 contains pages 00–57. These numbers identify the order in the public archive. They are not page numbers printed on every manuscript image. Use the release prefix when citing a page.

Three different representations
Original image: the preserved JPEG, including artwork, color, placement, and punctuation.
Archived transcription: a community-created symbolic representation of that image.
Known reading: a documented decoded or readable interpretation, shown separately when available.

Transcriptions cannot preserve every visual distinction. A delimiter in the text is not automatically proof of a particular printed mark or grammatical meaning.

Archive delimiter notation
Notation	Archive convention
-	Word separator
,	Three-dot symbol
.	Clause separator
&	Paragraph separator
$	Segment separator
§	Chapter separator
/	Line boundary
%	Page boundary

These names describe the transcription convention, not an independently established cipher function.

Corrections and measurements

The page record retains the archived text. Where a documented visual review identifies a correction, the record lists it explicitly and offers a separate reviewed-text view. Character offsets are zero-based positions in the archived Unicode text.

Rune counts and prime-value sums are calculated from the archived rune sequence. Decimal digits and punctuation do not contribute to these sums. Such totals describe the transcription; they are not proposed cipher keys.

Image coordinates

Recorded regions use the original JPEG pixels: x and y begin at the upper-left corner. Each rectangle is written as x, y, width, height. Cropped thumbnails are browsing aids; the full original and its checksum remain available.
